Local business website checklist: 12 things customers notice

By Piyush Pareek · Published 11 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Customers decide in seconds whether a local business looks trustworthy. Use this checklist on your own website, or on a competitor's, and fix anything you cannot tick. It is written for shops, clinics, hotels and service businesses in Bikaner, but it applies anywhere in India.

In this article
  1. Be found
  2. Load fast and work on a phone
  3. Earn trust
  4. Make contacting you easy
  5. Score yourself

Be found

  1. Claim your Google Business Profile. Fill in category, hours, photos and the same phone number as your site. This is what appears on Maps and in local searches.
  2. Keep name, address and phone identical everywhere. Differences between your site, Google and social profiles confuse both customers and search engines.
  3. Put your city in the page title and main heading. "Dentist in Bikaner" is clearer than "Welcome to our website".

Load fast and work on a phone

  1. Test on a real phone. Most local searches happen on mobile. Check text size, buttons and the menu.
  2. Keep it fast. Compress images, avoid heavy sliders and auto-playing video. Google measures this under Core Web Vitals (see web.dev/vitals).
  3. Use HTTPS. A "not secure" warning in the address bar sends people away.

Earn trust

  1. Show real photos and real work. Your shop, your team, your finished jobs. Stock photos feel generic.
  2. Show who is behind the business. A name, a face, an address and a way to reach you directly.
  3. Add reviews you genuinely have. Link to your Google reviews. Never publish invented testimonials.

Make contacting you easy

  1. Put a WhatsApp button where thumbs reach. Learn how in this WhatsApp enquiries guide.
  2. Show price or a clear starting point. Visitors who cannot find any price often leave. Compare how an honest price page works in the cost guide.
  3. Track where visitors come from. Use Google Analytics and UTM tags so you know which channel brings enquiries.

Score yourself

Count your ticks. Ten or more means your site is in good shape. Seven to nine means a few quick fixes will help. Below seven, the site is probably costing you customers every week.
